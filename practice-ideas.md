# Practice ideas: classic plays for AIGC

Use a short scene from a classic play to practice AI-generated content (AIGC). Test one main skill at a time: voice, facial expression, camera movement, visual style, or dialogue timing.

The ten plays below are useful starting points. The exercises are suggestions for this project. They are not quotations from the plays.

## Choose a text you can reuse

A work is in the **public domain** when copyright no longer applies to it in the relevant country. There is no worldwide rule that makes every play written before 1930 free to use. Publication date, author, edition, translation, and country can affect the result.

In the United States, copyright expired on works published in 1930 on 1 January 2026, subject to the applicable rules. This concerns publication, not the date of writing. In the United Kingdom, literary and dramatic works generally have protection for the author's life plus 70 years. Check the exact text you plan to use. See [Duke's 2026 public-domain guide](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026) and the [UK copyright duration guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term).

Project Gutenberg identifies the editions linked below as public domain in the USA. This does not establish permission in every country. Follow its [terms of use](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/terms_of_use.html) and check the edition's translator and other contributors where necessary.

## Tragedies and heavy drama

| Play and author | What to practice | Suggested exercise | Text source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Macbeth — William Shakespeare** | Dramatic voice, dark lighting, and atmospheric video prompts. | Make a short monologue scene. Keep the character and room consistent while tension increases. | [Macbeth](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1533) |
| **Hamlet — William Shakespeare** | Emotional pacing, pauses, and intense monologues. | Use a short passage. Make two versions with different pauses. Compare the emotional effect. | [Hamlet](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1524) |
| **Oedipus Rex — Sophocles** | Stylized ancient Greek drama, chorus structure, and epic storytelling. | Alternate one speaker with a chorus response. | **Faust — Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** | Surreal, demonic, and fantastical imagery. | Show one room before and after a supernatural change. Keep its layout and the character's identity consistent. | [Faust, Part 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14591), translated by Bayard Taylor |

## Comedies and satires

| Play and author | What to practice | Suggested exercise | Text source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Importance of Being Earnest — Oscar Wilde** | Witty dialogue, fast comic timing, and high-society settings. | Use a short exchange between two characters. | **The Miser — Molière** | Exaggerated expressions and physical comedy. | Show a character protecting a money box. Test eye movement, hand contact, and the timing of a physical reaction. | [The Miser](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6923), translated by Charles Heron Wall |

## Realism and social drama

| Play and author | What to practice | Suggested exercise | Text source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A Doll's House — Henrik Ibsen** | Realistic interaction, subtle expressions, and tense domestic dialogue. | Make a two-person conversation in one room. Use restrained gestures and a clear emotional change. | [A Doll's House](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2542), translated by R. Farquharson Sharp |
| **The Cherry Orchard — Anton Chekhov** | Melancholy, ensemble scenes, and meaning beneath the spoken words. | Start with two characters and one silent observer. | **Pygmalion — George Bernard Shaw** | Accent changes, speech rhythm, and character transformation. | Record the same short passage with two original voice performances. Keep the character's appearance consistent. | [Pygmalion](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3825) |

Start with **A Doll's House** for subtle acting, **Macbeth** for atmosphere, or **The Importance of Being Earnest** for dialogue timing. Keep the first exercise small: one location, one or two speaking characters, and a few shots.

## Rules for AI generation and YouTube

### 1. Check the exact text

Use an original public-domain text or a translation whose status you have checked. A modern translation, adaptation, screenplay, introduction, or illustration can have separate copyright. A public-domain play does not give permission to copy a recent film adaptation.

Record the title, author, translator, edition, source link, and date checked. Review rights for any additional material you copy. See the [UK guidance on copyright duration and separate works](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term).

### 2. Create new assets or use assets with permission

Create your own character designs, voices, music, and visuals, or use assets under suitable licenses. Use original voice direction instead of cloning a famous living actor without permission. Do not copy frames, recordings, or music from a modern film, such as Kenneth Branagh's *Hamlet*, simply because its underlying play is public domain.

Treat “100% AI-generated” as a description of the process. It is not proof that an output is free of third-party rights. Keep records of asset sources, permissions, and licenses. YouTube explains the separate rights that can apply to audiovisual works and sound recordings in its [copyright guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466).

### 3. Check your AI tool's terms

Before commercial distribution, check the terms for the exact tool and plan you use. Confirm that they permit your intended use, including a monetized YouTube channel. Check output licenses, attribution requirements, and restrictions on uploaded references. Free and paid plans can have different terms; do not assume either gives all necessary rights.

### 4. Follow YouTube's requirements

No process guarantees zero copyright claims or strikes. Public-domain material can still receive a claim, and you may need evidence to dispute it. See [YouTube's copyright guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466).

Use the required AI-content disclosure when your video falls within YouTube's policy. The requirement depends on the content, including relevant realistic generated or altered media. See [YouTube's AI-content disclosure policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).

## Use these ideas with the project skills

1. Select a short passage. Record its source and the edition you checked. Decide whether to use the wording directly or write your own adaptation.
2. Use `director-advisor` to turn the passage into a storyboard. Give the emotional change, location, target length, and speaking characters.
3. Use `ai-drama-reference-art` for character and scene reference prompts. Generate the actual reference images before the video stage. Use `ksrmj` when you want to explore a scene image, poster, or VFX look.
4. Use `seedance-clip-writer` with the approved storyboard, character descriptions, palette, and actual reference assets. Specify your platform and dialogue language. Check the platform's current limits before generation.
5. Generate one version. Review identity consistency, visible actions, dialogue timing, and sound. Change one or two variables for the next attempt.

Save scene work under `outputs/<scene>/`. For example, use `outputs/macbeth-practice/` for a Macbeth exercise. Read [introduction.md](introduction.md) for the skill commands and workflow.

Example request:

```text
Use director-advisor to storyboard the Macbeth passage I provide.
Target: a 20-second practice scene with one character and four shots.
Focus on voice pacing and dark interior lighting.
Then write character and scene reference prompts.
Save the documents under outputs/macbeth-practice/.
Mark reference images as pending until I supply the actual files.
```

The duration and shot count above are practice targets, not verified platform limits. This guide does not install tools or generate media.
